The Aerospace & Defence Commission

The Aerospace & Defence Commission consists of a group of talented young people from diverse academic and professional backgrounds with a strong interest in defence matters. We are convinced that defence and security are key elements for Europe's stability, credibility and strategic autonomy.

 What We Do


The Aerospace & Defence Commission is dedicated to producing high-quality analysis and papers focused on specific issues concerning the defence sector. Our activities include:

  • In-depth research and analysis of the challenges and opportunities of the European defence sector.
  • Development of innovative and concrete policy proposals based on scientific evidence and empirical data.
  • Organizing events and workshops to promote debate and exchange of ideas among young people, experts and policymakers.
  • Collaboration with other VOYCE committees and external entities, whether industry or institutional, to broaden the perspective and impact of our analyses.
  • Presentation of our analyses and proposals to policymakers in Brussels and other European contexts, with the goal of positively influencing policy decisions on defence.

Meet The Commissioner:

Michela Miglio

Michela Miglio is a dedicated and ambitious student pursuing a Bachelor's degree in International Politics and Government at Bocconi University. Her main interest lies in how the defense sector is shaped by the interplay between public and private actors - from the role of antitrust and M&A in structuring markets to how corporate strategy and private capital can strengthen national defense capabilities. She has deepened this focus through both academic and professional experiences, including an internship at the Italian Ministry of Enterprises, where she contributed to projects on defense industrial policy and the application of golden power regulations. Michela also founded Praesidium, a magazine dedicated to defense and cybersecurity created in collaboration with a former Italian Army general. She has served as Head of Public Affairs at a Bocconi-based think tank on defense and geopolitics, leading a team that organized high-level events with experts and policymakers. In addition, she organizes career service initiatives within a student representative association, fostering connections between students and major firms. Driven by a strong commitment to European security and industrial cooperation, Michela is determined to contribute to a more integrated and competitive European defense ecosystem.

Marie Helene Olafsen is a Politics and International Relations student at the London School of Economics, studying across Lisbon, Paris, and Berlin through Forward College. Her studies focus on international political theory, security, and foreign policy, with a particular interest in how strategic narratives shape military interventions and global decision-making. This interest has developed through fieldwork in Vietnam and Ghana, where she explored how legacies of conflict and perceptions of development influence political legitimacy and civilian resilience. Alongside her academic work, Marie has gained hands-on experience through humanitarian support roles with the Norwegian Red Cross and field-based research in regions shaped by the long-term impacts of conflict. These grassroots experiences continue to shape her curiosity about diplomacy, democratic governance, and the human dimensions of international security.

Miguel Daza Céspedes is a final-year student completing a Double Bachelor's in Business Analytics and International Relations at Universidad Pontificia Comillas (ICADE) in Madrid. His work sits at the intersection of quantitative analysis and European security policy, with a particular focus on nuclear deterrence and proliferation dynamics. His research centres on how U.S. military interventions shaped the strategic calculus of non-nuclear states; arguing that the fates of Iraq and Libya signalled to regimes worldwide that nuclear capability represents the only reliable guarantee of survival against external coercion. This comparative framework informs his broader interest in how deterrence theory intersects with contemporary questions of European strategic autonomy and great power competition. He has built a professional background spanning data analytics, geopolitical risk analysis, quantitative finance, and cybersecurity consulting, including work on cross-sector engagement projects aligned with Saudi Vision 2030 at Metyis. He is also the developer of MERIDIAN, an independent OSINT platform integrating geospatial intelligence, supply chain monitoring, and disinformation detection, a project that reflects his commitment to bridging the gap between data-driven methods and strategic decision-making. Miguel will pursue the MA in War Studies at King's College London and is committed to contributing analytical rigour to the policy debates shaping Europe's security architecture.
